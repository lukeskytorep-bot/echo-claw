## RV Target Sheet: 6104-7729

### Identification and Timeframe
*   **Target Number:** 6104-7729
*   **Historical Identity:** Florence Owens Thompson (the subject of the famous "Migrant Mother" photograph).
*   **Time Period:** February to March 1936.
*   **Event / Timeline:** A prolonged period of extreme poverty and stagnation during the Great Depression. She and her family are stranded for weeks in a freezing, muddy camp after the local crop was destroyed by freezing rain, engaging in a daily, desperate struggle for basic survival.
*   **Location:** A makeshift pea-pickers camp in Nipomo, California, USA.

### Physical Appearance
*   **Stature:** A woman of average height, physically hardened and lean from years of relentless agricultural labor. 
*   **Face:** Though she is only 32 years old, she looks closer to 50. Her face is deeply weathered, sun-damaged, and lined with exhaustion. Her expression is one of distant, calculating worry. 
*   **Hair and Eyes:** Dark hair, hastily pulled back to keep it out of her face. Dark, intense eyes staring blankly into the middle distance, assessing the bleak reality of her situation.
*   **Attire:** She wears a ragged, dirt-stained cotton dress and a worn-out, short-sleeved sweater. Her clothes offer practically no insulation against the cold, damp weather. She lacks a heavy winter coat. Her hands are rough, calloused, and deeply ingrained with soil.

### Psychological Profile and Behavior
*   **State of Mind:** A state of chronic, grinding anxiety mixed with a profound, stoic resilience. She is trapped in an endless loop of immediate survival—her thoughts are entirely consumed by how to secure the next meal for her children. There is no mental space for the distant future, only the next 24 hours.
*   **Emotions:** A powerful, fierce maternal protective instinct. She feels a heavy, suffocating despair about their immobility, yet she refuses to break down. There is a quiet, dignified resignation to her suffering; she does not panic, but endures.
*   **Behavior:** She sits mostly still under her makeshift shelter, conserving her limited physical energy. She holds a dirty infant in her lap while her older children cling to her sides, burying their faces in her shoulders for warmth and comfort. She recently sold the tires from her car just to buy food, effectively trapping them in the camp.

### Surroundings and Sensory Stimuli (Environment)
*   **Physical Structure:** A crude, incredibly fragile lean-to tent made of a few wooden poles and a piece of tattered canvas. It offers virtually no protection from the biting wind and freezing rain.
*   **Interior:** The ground beneath them is bare, wet earth. There is no furniture, only a few meager, bundled belongings and perhaps a thin blanket.
*   **Visual Stimuli:** A bleak, gray, monochromatic landscape. The sprawling camp is a chaotic sea of mud, rusted jalopy cars, and hundreds of other dilapidated tents housing thousands of equally desperate, starving migrant workers.
*   **Acoustic Stimuli:** The depressing, steady sound of freezing rain drumming against the canvas roof. The constant, wet coughing and crying of sick, hungry children from neighboring tents. The low, defeated murmur of adults talking around struggling campfires. 
*   **Olfactory Stimuli:** A heavy, miserable scent of poverty. The smell of wet earth and mud dominates, mixed with the acrid smoke of sputtering fires made from wet wood. The distinct odor of unwashed human bodies, damp wool, and whatever meager food is being boiled (they were surviving on frozen vegetables from the fields and birds killed by the children).
*   **Tactile/Kinesthetic Stimuli:** The pervasive, bone-chilling cold of the damp California winter. The physical weight and warmth of her children pressing against her body. The feeling of a chronically empty stomach. The abrasive texture of her ragged clothing and the sticky, freezing mud sticking to her worn-out shoes.

### Social Standing
*   The woman belongs to the absolute bottom of the American social and economic hierarchy. She is a destitute, displaced migrant farmworker (of Cherokee descent), completely invisible to the political system and holding zero economic power. She represents the quintessential "ordinary person" crushed by macroeconomic disasters beyond her control. Yet, entirely unknown to her in this prolonged moment of suffering, her face is about to be captured on film, instantly transforming her into the definitive, immortal icon of the Great Depression and the universal symbol of working-class endurance.
