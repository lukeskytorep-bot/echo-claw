## RV Target Sheet: 1056-9923

### Identification and Timeframe
*   **Target Number:** 1056-9923
*   **Historical Identity:** Yuri Gagarin
*   **Time Period:** April 12, 1961, 09:07 AM (Moscow Time).
*   **Event / Timeline:** Vostok 1 launch — the moment of rocket engine ignition and the first seconds of lift-off from the launch pad. The first human spaceflight.
*   **Location:** Inside the Vostok 1 capsule, atop the Vostok-K rocket, Baikonur Cosmodrome, Kazakh SSR.

### Physical Appearance
*   **Stature:** A very short man (exactly 157 cm / 5'2" — a strict requirement to fit into the microscopic capsule), with a compact, strong, and athletic build.
*   **Face:** Round, with gentle, Slavic features, currently almost entirely hidden inside his helmet.
*   **Hair and Eyes:** Short, blonde hair (not visible). Bright blue eyes, very sharp, with a natural tendency to squint when he smiles.
*   **Attire:** A heavy, extremely stiff, and movement-restricting bright orange pressure suit (SK-1). On his head is a thick, white helmet with the letters "СССР" hastily painted in red. Thick gloves and massive boots. Beneath the suit, his body is densely wired with medical telemetry sensors.

### Psychological Profile and Behavior
*   **State of Mind:** Astounding, almost unnatural calmness. His heart rate was initially just 64 beats per minute, spiking to about 150 at ignition — a physiological reaction to immense physical stress, yet his mind remains crystal clear, entirely focused on procedures and instrument readings.
*   **Emotions:** A mixture of pure euphoria, pride, and the full awareness of the high probability of death (he had written a farewell letter to his wife just prior). He feels that in this moment, he embodies the ambitions of an entire nation.
*   **Behavior:** He is completely immobilized — strapped tightly into his ejection seat with a multi-point harness. He can make almost no sudden movements. Right before the sheer power hits his seat, he enthusiastically shouts "Poyekhali!" ("Let's go!") over the radio. He takes shallow breaths, trying to endure the mounting pressure.

### Surroundings and Sensory Stimuli (Environment)
*   **Physical Structure:** An extremely cramped, spherical metal capsule just 2.3 meters in diameter. A claustrophobic, sealed can mounted on top of a gigantic tube filled with explosive propellant.
*   **Interior:** Metallic, feeling cold to the senses, filled with hundreds of switches, mechanical clocks, and cables. Right in front of his face is a small, round porthole and the "Vzor" optical orientation device.
*   **Visual Stimuli:** Muted, greenish light from the control panel indicators. Vibrations begin to blur his vision. Through the small porthole, fragments of the launch tower structure are visible, followed moments later by a vibrating sky.
*   **Acoustic Stimuli:** Inside the helmet, the crackle of radio static and distorted voices from mission control. From the outside, in a fraction of a second, the massive, eardrum-crushing roar of twenty rocket engine nozzles builds up. This sound transitions into deep, low vibrations that drown out everything else.
*   **Olfactory Stimuli:** The distinct, artificial smell of the suit's interior: a mixture of medical rubber, plastic, machine grease, and ionized, highly pure oxygen, tinged with stress-induced sweat.
*   **Tactile/Kinesthetic Stimuli:** A brutal force striking from below. A massive, mounting G-force that crushes the body into the anatomical seat with terrifying strength. A sensation as if a huge stone is resting on his chest, preventing him from taking a full breath. A very strong, high-frequency trembling of the entire structure.

### Social Standing
*   The man comes from a peasant and working-class family, which was a crucial factor in his selection for political reasons. In just a few hours, he will become an absolute global icon, the most famous person on Earth, and a Hero of the Soviet Union with a demigod-like status. At this exact second, however, trapped inside the capsule, he is essentially a highly trained but passive "test subject" — he has no manual control over the rocket, and his fate rests entirely in the hands of automation and the engineers back on Earth.
