# Target 0027

**Target ID:** 542F WK19  
**Date of the target:** December 17, 1903 (approx. 10:35 a.m. local time)  

**Target:** The first successful flight of a powered, controlled, heavier-than-air aircraft (the Wright Flyer) at Kill Devil Hills, North Carolina, USA (36° 1′ 1″ N, 75° 40′ 7″ W).

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a groundbreaking historical and technological event. The scene captures the exact moment an experimental, fragile mechanical structure achieves initial lift against gravity. The environment is a barren, sandy coastal landscape characterized by cold, harsh weather conditions and a stark absence of modern infrastructure, with human activity focused intensely on the fragile flying machine.

---

### Part 1: The Experimental Aircraft (The Wright Flyer)

*   **The Structure:** The central object is a primitive aircraft built with a biplane configuration (two large, parallel horizontal wings). The framework is constructed from lightweight wood (spruce and ash) and is tightly covered in a light-colored, unbleached muslin fabric.
*   **Aeronautical Components:** The vehicle features a forward-mounted twin elevator (a canard) for pitch control and a rear-mounted vertical twin rudder. 
*   **Propulsion:** A heavy, rudimentary gasoline engine is mounted asymmetrically on the lower wing. It drives two large, wooden pusher propellers rotating in opposite directions at the rear of the wings, connected via bicycle-style chains.
*   **Kinetic State:** The aircraft is captured in a state of low-altitude, low-speed kinetic flight, hovering less than two feet above the sandy surface.

### Part 2: The Human Element and Infrastructure

*   **The Pilot:** A human figure (Orville Wright) is positioned directly on the aircraft. He is not seated, but rather lying prone (flat on his stomach) in a cradle on the lower wing, manipulating the wing-warping controls with his hips.
*   **The Observer:** A second human figure (Wilbur Wright) is actively running alongside the right wing of the airborne craft. He is wearing a dark suit and a flat cap. He has just let go of the wing, having stabilized the aircraft during its initial ground roll.
*   **The Launch Mechanism:** Behind the aircraft, stretching diagonally across the flat sand, is a long, straight wooden monorail track (a single rail constructed of four-by-fours with a metal strip). The aircraft utilized this rail as a smooth surface to achieve takeoff speed, leaving behind a small wooden launching dolly.

### Part 3: The Environment and Atmosphere

*   **The Terrain:** The ground is completely flat, devoid of vegetation, and composed entirely of cold, loose beach sand.
*   **Weather Conditions:** The visual and historical data indicate a bleak, freezing winter morning. The sky is overcast and grey. 
*   **Atmospheric Dynamics:** There is a constant, stiff, and freezing headwind blowing across the dunes (exceeding 20 miles per hour). This harsh wind is a critical dynamic element, generating the necessary lift over the fabric wings to allow the underpowered machine to achieve flight.
