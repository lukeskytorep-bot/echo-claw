# Target 0029

**Target ID:** 491B KZ22  
**Date of the target:** February 23, 1945  

**Target:** United States Marines raising the American flag atop Mount Suribachi during the Battle of Iwo Jima (24° 45′ 13″ N, 141° 17′ 23″ E).

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a highly recognized historical military event occurring on the peak of a volcanic island. The scene captures a group of six human figures engaged in intense, synchronized physical labor to hoist a heavy vertical pole bearing a large fabric flag. The environment is characterized by blasted, barren rocky terrain and the chaotic aftermath of combat.

---

### Part 1: The Terrain and Geological Features

*   **The Ground:** The immediate environment is the summit of a dormant volcanic cone. The surface is uneven, sloping, and entirely composed of loose, dark volcanic ash, fragmented rocks, and combat debris. 
*   **The Elevation:** The location is a high-vantage point (approximately 169 meters above sea level), offering a commanding, panoramic view of the surrounding island and the ocean below.
*   **Vegetation:** The terrain is completely barren, devoid of any natural vegetation or organic ground cover due to the volcanic nature of the island and recent heavy bombardment.

### Part 2: The Human Subjects and Kinetic Activity

*   **The Figures:** Six human subjects (U.S. Marines and a Navy corpsman) are clustered tightly together on the uneven ground. 
*   **Attire and Gear:** They are dressed in standard-issue WWII military combat fatigues, heavy boots, and steel helmets. Several figures carry visible combat gear, including utility belts, canteens, and holstered weapons.
*   **The Action:** The group is captured in a moment of extreme, synchronized physical exertion. Their bodies are angled forward, leaning heavily into a central object. Their arms are raised and extended, gripping the object to push it upward against gravity.

### Part 3: The Central Object (Pole and Flag)

*   **The Pole:** The focal point of the physical exertion is a long, heavy, salvaged iron water pipe. At the exact moment captured, the pole is positioned at an approximate 45-degree angle to the ground, midway through being erected vertically.
*   **The Flag:** Attached securely to the upper end of the iron pole is a large, rectangular fabric flag. 
*   **Atmospheric Interaction:** A strong breeze is actively blowing, causing the fabric of the flag to unfurl and flap dynamically in the wind as the pole is being hoisted.
