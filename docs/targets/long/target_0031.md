# Target 0031

**Target ID:** 228G CT42  
**Date of the target:** Present day (Facility operational from 1930; largely obsolete by late 20th century)  

**Target:** The M42 secret sub-basement and electrical power station, located approximately 100 feet beneath Grand Central Terminal, New York City.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a massive, hidden subterranean industrial infrastructure. The scene captures a highly secure, restricted, and obsolete electrical power station buried deep within the bedrock of Manhattan. The environment is characterized by monumental, heavy-duty early 20th-century electrical machinery and vast mechanical control boards, existing in stark contrast to the opulent, bustling passenger concourses located directly above it.

---

### Part 1: The Subterranean Environment

*   **The Location:** The facility, known as M42, occupies a four-story underground void measuring approximately 250 by 50 feet. It is situated roughly 100 feet beneath street level (specifically under the Graybar Building).
*   **The Architecture:** The environment is strictly utilitarian and industrial. The walls are composed of unpainted, grimy concrete and exposed brick. Heavy metal exit doors are secured and surrounded by prominent industrial signage, including "CAUTION HARD HAT AREA" and a large, weathered sign reading "NOTICE: RESTRICTED AREA AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY". 
*   **The Status:** While originally a highly guarded, critical piece of infrastructure (particularly during World War II), the facility was largely sealed off from the public and rendered obsolete by solid-state technology in the late 20th century. It remains static, preserving the original equipment in a state of historical dormancy.

### Part 2: The Power Conversion Machinery

*   **Rotary Converters:** The physical core of the space is dominated by a series of massive rotary converters. These are colossal, heavy circular machines constructed of cast iron, thick copper wiring, and large magnetic coils. 
*   **The Function:** These machines were designed for extreme kinetic and electrical loads, originally required to transform incoming high-voltage Alternating Current (AC) into the Direct Current (DC) necessary to power the massive rail network above.
*   **Scale and Mass:** The exposed stators and rotors of the machinery feature highly repetitive, tightly packed geometric patterns of copper windings, showcasing the immense scale of early industrial electrical engineering.

### Part 3: Command and Control Apparatus

*   **The Control Boards:** Lining the walls of the facility are expansive, black metal control panels designed for manual operation and monitoring.
*   **Instrumentation:** The boards are densely packed with analog technology. They feature rows of manual toggle levers, large rotary knobs, backlit warning panels, and glass-faced gauges measuring electrical current and voltage.
*   **Signage and Interface:** Specialized placards and warning lights are bolted to the panels. Notable indicators include a prominent "DANGER HIGH VOLTAGE" sign over a grid of illuminated square tiles with handwritten markings (e.g., "UNIT ONE CELL FAILURE", "FIRE IN SUBSTATION", "AIR SUPPLY FAILURE"). Another desk features a large manual knob labeled "EMERGENCY TRIP RECTIFIER 4 O.C.B."
