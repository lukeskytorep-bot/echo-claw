# Target 0025

**Target coordinates:** 56rt yt65  
**Date of the target:** Active between February 1986 and March 2001.  

**Target:** The Soviet/Russian modular space station *Mir* - Low Earth orbit (LEO). 

**Description:**
The target is a massive, highly complex artificial habitat operating in a microgravity environment. It represents a historic, long-term orbital outpost constructed from multiple independent cylindrical modules joined together in space. The visual profile is characterized by a somewhat chaotic, asymmetrical geometry of metallic cylinders, docking nodes, and numerous expansive solar arrays extending outward in multiple directions. The structure is set against the absolute vacuum of space and the bright, curved horizon of the Earth.

---

### Part 1: Architecture and Modular Assembly

*   **The Core Module (DOS-7):** The structural heart of the station is a central cylindrical habitation block, measuring over 13 meters in length. It features a multiple docking adapter at its forward end (equipped with five ports) and serves as the primary living quarters and control center. 
*   **The Expansion Modules:** Connected to the central core is an array of specialized scientific and logistical modules (*Kvant-1, Kvant-2, Kristall, Spektr,* and *Priroda*). These modules are arranged in a radial, cross-like configuration around the primary docking node, giving the station a dense, highly irregular footprint.
*   **Mass and Scale:** At its peak configuration, the interconnected metallic structure spanned over 30 meters in both length and width, weighing approximately 135 tons and representing the largest artificial object in orbit prior to the International Space Station.

### Part 2: Energy Collection and Docking Infrastructure, and Crew Capacity

*   **Crew Complement:** The station typically housed a primary long-duration crew of **3 astronauts/cosmonauts** for standard long-term expeditions. During crew handovers, short-term visiting missions (including Space Shuttle docking flights), the population temporarily expanded, reaching up to **6 to 10 people** on board simultaneously. In total, the station was visited by 96 individual crew members throughout its operational life.
*   **Solar Arrays:** Extending outward from the various cylindrical modules are numerous large, flat, rectangular solar panels. Some modules feature standard dual lateral panels, while others (such as the *Spektr* module) display an X-shaped configuration of four arrays. These panels provide a highly reflective, geometric contrast to the main cylindrical hulls.
*   **Docking Mechanisms:** The outer extremities of the complex feature specialized mechanical docking ports. Visual data shows a visiting spacecraft (a Soyuz or Progress vehicle, distinguishable by its spherical orange orbital module and dark lower section) physically hard-docked to one of the longitudinal ports. 
*   **The Shuttle Docking Module:** An elongated cylindrical extension specifically designed for the American Space Shuttle is attached to the *Kristall* module, bridging the gap between Soviet/Russian and American aerospace engineering.

### Part 3: The Orbital Environment

*   **The Vacuum:** The station is suspended in the lethal, zero-pressure vacuum of low Earth orbit. 
*   **The Backdrop:** The visual data contrasts the highly engineered, brightly illuminated metallic surfaces of the station against two distinct backgrounds: the deep, impenetrable blackness of deep space, and the vivid blue oceans and white atmospheric cloud formations of the planetary surface directly below.
