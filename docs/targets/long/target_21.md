# Target 0021

**Target coordinates:**   3274y yw72  
**Date of the target:** Active between May 1973 and February 1974. (Low Earth orbit.)  

**Target:** Skylab, the United States' first space station.

**Description:**
The target is a massive, multi-modular artificial structure operating in a microgravity environment. It is the United States' first space station, constructed primarily from a repurposed Saturn V third stage (S-IVB). The station is a complex geometric assembly consisting of a large central orbital workshop, a solar observatory, and forward docking modules. It operated in low Earth orbit, where astronaut crews conducted spacewalks, Earth observations, and hundreds of scientific experiments.

---

### Part 1: The Main Hull (Orbital Workshop)

*   **The Structure:** The core of the station is a massive cylindrical orbital workshop, which originally served as a Saturn V third stage. This central module boasts a pressurized volume of 270 cubic meters. The entire station has a mass of 199,750 pounds (90,610 kg) when the 31,000-pound (14,000 kg) Apollo command and service module (CSM) is docked.
*   **Exterior Features:** Visual data shows the main cylinder partially covered by a rectangular, golden-colored thermal shield draped over its hull. Extending laterally from the main workshop is a single, large rectangular solar array panel.
*   **Aft Section:** The rear of the station houses a large waste tank. It also contains propellant tanks used for maneuvering jets, as well as a heat radiator.

### Part 2: Forward Modules and Observatory

*   **Docking and Airlock:** Attached to the forward end of the main workshop is an airlock module equipped with extravehicular activity (EVA) hatches. This section connects to a multiple docking adapter that features two separate docking ports.
*   **Apollo Telescope Mount:** Mounted to the forward section is the Apollo Telescope Mount, which functions as a multi-spectral solar observatory. Visually, this module is distinguished by its four large solar arrays deployed in a symmetrical "windmill" or "X" configuration.
*   **The Docked Spacecraft:** An Apollo command and service module (CSM) is shown docked to the station's adapter. This attached spacecraft was used to deliver the three-astronaut crews to the station. It also provided electrical power to the station via its fuel cells.

### Part 3: The Orbital Environment

*   **The Backdrop:** The massive metallic structure is suspended in the vacuum of low Earth orbit. Visual data contrasts the stark, highly reflective geometry of the station against the absolute black void of space, as well as the bright, cloud-covered curved surface of the Earth below.
