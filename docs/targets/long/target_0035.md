# Target 0035

**Target ID:** 902B NF88  
**Date of the target:** Present day (with structures dating to 1626).  

**Target:** The Blue Nile Falls (Tis Abay) on the Blue Nile river (South Gondar, Amhara Region, Ethiopia).

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a major waterfall system and nearby historic infrastructure in Ethiopia. The environment is characterized by a massive drop of water over a wide cliff face, generating dense mist, surrounded by lush vegetation and significant ecological and historical boundaries.

---

### Part 1: The Waterfall and Geology

*   The central feature is a waterfall dropping 42 metres (138 ft) over a rugged rock face on the upper course of the Blue Nile river.
*   The cascade is composed of four distinct streams.
*   The volume of the falls fluctuates dramatically, expanding to over 400 metres (1,300 ft) wide during the rainy season and reducing to a trickle during the dry season.

### Part 2: The Atmospheric Environment

*   The immense volume of falling water creates a thick, persistent cloud of spray and mist in the gorge below.
*   This prominent visual feature gives the falls its local Amharic name, "Tis Abay," which translates to "great smoke".
*   The dense mist frequently interacts with sunlight to create large, highly visible rainbows spanning across the river gorge.

### Part 3: Ecology and Infrastructure

*   The geological drop of the falls acts as a physical barrier that isolates the ecology of Lake Tana from the rest of the Nile, playing a role in the evolution of the lake's endemic fauna.
*   Since 2003, the natural flow has been altered by a nearby hydro-electric station, which takes much of the flow out of the falls except during the rainy season.
*   A short distance downstream stands a historic, multi-arched stone bridge spanning the river gorge. 
*   Built in 1626 by command of Emperor Susenyos and supervised by a craftsman from India, it holds the distinction of being the first stone bridge constructed in Ethiopia.
