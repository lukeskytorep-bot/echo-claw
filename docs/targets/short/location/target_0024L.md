# Target: Great Barrier Reef

The presented target is the Great Barrier Reef, located along the northeastern coast of Australia in the Coral Sea. It is the largest single structure on Earth made by living organisms (visible from space), inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1981.

## Environment and Structure:

* **Terrain and extent:** The reef stretches over 2,300 km (from the Torres Strait to Lady Elliot Island) and covers an area of over 346,000 km². It is not a uniform entity but a system consisting of over 3,000 individual reefs and about 1,000 islands. Shallow underwater coral structures (e.g., Kangaroo Reef, Wallaby Reef) are visible within the target area, contrasting with bright turquoise against the deep navy blue of the surrounding ocean.
* **Geology:** The reef has been forming for millions of years from the accumulation of calcareous skeletons of corals. Its current shape began to form about 9,000 years ago on the remnants of older structures, under specific conditions: water temperatures above 18°C (optimally 26–27°C), high transparency, depth up to 50 m, and salinity of 30–40‰.

## Fauna and Flora:

The ecosystem of the Great Barrier Reef is characterized by gigantic biodiversity.
* **Animals:** These waters are inhabited by about 600 species of corals, 1,625 species of fish, and thousands of mollusks and echinoderms (e.g., the blue starfish *Linckia laevigata* visible in the photo). 
* **Protected species:** There are many rare and endangered species here, including sea turtles (e.g., the visible green sea turtle), crocodiles, dugongs, various species of sharks (e.g., great white shark, whale shark), and whales (e.g., humpback whale, blue whale).
* **Biological phenomena:** Symbiotic relationships are common on the reef, such as corals with zooxanthellae algae (which provide them with food and color), or goby fish with blind shrimp of the Alpheidae family.
