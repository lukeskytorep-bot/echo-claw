# Target: The Incan Citadel of Machu Picchu

The presented target is an ancient, highly preserved urban and ceremonial complex built by the Inca Empire, situated in the Andes Mountains at an elevation of approximately 9,060 feet. Constructed in the early 1400s and rediscovered in 1911, the site is completely integrated into a dramatic natural environment and functioned as a secret, self-contained city.

## Environment and Topography:

* **Mountain Isolation:** The 5-square-mile citadel is located on a high mountain ridge, rendering it completely invisible from the valley floor below. It is encircled by steep, often cloud-shrouded Andean peaks, with the prominent peak of Wayna Picchu towering directly behind the main ruins.
* **Deep Valley and River:** Approximately 2,000 feet below the ruins lies a deep gorge through which the roaring Urubamba River flows. Access to the summit from the forested valley floor is achieved via a remarkably steep, sharply zigzagging dirt switchback road carved into the mountainside.
* **Agricultural Integration:** The steep mountain slopes surrounding the urban core are heavily modified with an extensive system of stepped agricultural terraces. These terraces, supported by solid stone retaining walls, were designed to provide self-sufficient food production for the population and are watered by natural mountain springs.

## Architecture and Urban Infrastructure:

* **City Layout:** The settlement consists of approximately 150 distinct structures, including houses, palaces, baths, temples, and storage rooms, arranged across various topographical levels. Some of the smaller stone buildings on the higher terraces have been reconstructed with traditional thatched roofs to reflect their original appearance.
* **Megalithic Stonework:** The structures are constructed from local gray granite using extraordinary engineering techniques. Irregularly shaped stone blocks, some weighing 50 tons or more, are precisely sculpted and fitted together without the use of mortar. The joints are executed with such extreme exactitude that not even a thin knife blade can be inserted between the stones.

## Ceremonial and Astronomical Features:

* **The Intihuatana Stone:** A central feature of the ruins is the Intihuatana (meaning "Hitching Post of the Sun"), a highly sacred, vertically carved gray stone pillar set on a broader, multi-angled base. It functioned as a precise astronomical observatory. At midday during the two equinoxes (March 21st and September 21st), the sun stands directly above the pillar, casting zero shadow. This marked ceremonial periods where the Inca conceptually "tied" the sun to halt its northward movement.
* **Historical Preservation:** Because the Spanish conquerors never discovered this secret city, the highly sacred Intihuatana stone remains completely intact in its original position. The site also features specific alignments with the December solstice and includes a cavern on Wayna Picchu known as the "Temple of the Moon."
* **Site Demographics:** Osteological examination of skeletal remains found at the site reveals an equal ratio of male to female bones, indicating it functioned as a diverse ceremonial and residential center rather than an exclusive sanctuary for women or priestesses.
