# Target: The Fribourg Ice Palace (Karl Neuhaus Installation, Fribourg, Switzerland )  

The presented target is the Fribourg Ice Palace, an elaborate winter fantasy environment constructed annually entirely out of ice and snow by sculptor Karl Neuhaus. Operational only during the freezing winter months from Christmas until early March, the transient complex forms an immersive village scale installation of frozen architecture, caves, and organic sculptures integrated into a mountainous landscape.

## Macro Infrastructure and Architecture:

* **The Ice Fortress:** Dominating the slope of the mountain is a monumental castle built completely from ice blocks and packed snow. The fortress features defined towers, battlements, defensive walls, and grand, illuminated staircases climbing the terrain.
* **Subterranean Caves and Tunnels:** The interior features a network of pedestrian tunnels, hollowed ice rooms, and curved domes. The ceilings of these corridors are heavily packed with thousands of long, sharp, white icicles hanging down to form frozen arcades. 
* **The Palace Gate:** The entrance to the winter park is marked by a massive ice archway. The pillars and upper curved beam are uformed by dense clusters of thick vertical icicles, creating a cavernous portal into the grounds.

## Sculptural Elements and Organic Forms:

* **Frozen Geysers:** Rising vertically from the snow fields are tall, conical ice pillars mimicking frozen geysers, natural stalagmites, or stylized trees. These structures exhibit a highly irregular, coarse, and rough micro-impulsive texture.
* **Exotic Frozen Vegetation:** The palace grounds feature custom ice carvings shaped like sprawling palm trees or deep flower chalices erupting directly out of the passive snow cover. 
* **Internal Neon Integration:** The organic ice shoots and stems are engineered with hollow internal channels holding technical neon tubes and wiring, allowing the light source to project directly through the translucent medium of the ice.

## Illumination and Chromatic Composition:

The entire complex is characterized by a high-contrast, surreal night-time illumination scheme that uses color to define distinct spatial boundaries:

* **Dual-Tone Tunnels:** Walking passages feature a stark chromatic split, where the central corridors drown in a cold, white-blue glare while the lateral ice blocks are backlit by a warm, fiery orange-red and yellow light.
* **Neon Dominance:** Specific towers, stalagmites, and artistic elements are spectacularly lit from within by saturated neon green, intense pink-magenta, and deep purple-fiolet glares.
* **Linear Pathway Guides:** Safe transit paths are demarcated by linear bands of warm yellow light embedded directly into the vertical ice walls, tracing the zaśnieżone paths.

## Environment and Human Activity:

* **Natural Border:** The artificial ice structures stand in high contrast to the surrounding environment, which consists of a dark forested hillside and tall, mature spruce trees heavily weighed down by thick layers of winter snow.
* **Pedestrian Interactivity:** The park operates as an active tourist hub at night. Visitors dressed in heavy winter clothing, winter hats, and backpacks travel along the designated paths, exploring the interiors of the houses and using mobile devices to document the landscape under a dark night sky.
