# Target 0034L

**Target coordinates:** 134u 93ue  
**Date of the target:** November 2021 (with historical context from 2009)  

**Target:** Wat Pa Maha Chedi Kaew (Wat Lan Kuad / Temple of a Million Bottles) - Khun Han, Sisaket, Thailand. Eco-friendly religious complex.

**Description:**
The target is a highly unusual, intricately constructed religious and cultural complex set within a lush, tropical environment. The defining characteristic of this site is its primary building material: approximately 1.5 million recycled glass beer bottles. The rigid, repetitive geometry of the bottles (predominantly green and brown glass) replaces traditional bricks or wood, creating highly textured, semi-translucent facades that reflect sunlight. The main focal point is a multi-tiered, spire-topped temple pavilion elevated on concrete pillars directly over a rectangular, man-made reflecting pool. The surrounding grounds consist of wide, flat concrete courtyards, secondary pavilions, and dense tropical tree canopies. 

---

### Detailed Scene Breakdown

#### Architecture and Unique Materials
*   **The Main Temple (Over Water):** A striking, multi-tiered structure built on stilts over a rectangular pond. The sweeping, steeply pitched roof is entirely clad in green glass bottles, while the main walls utilize a patterned mix of brown and green bottles set in concrete mortar. The structure features arched walkways and traditional Thai architectural spires (chedi).
*   **Secondary Structures:** The complex includes over 20 other buildings, including open-air pavilions, a crematorium, and visitor toilets. The pillars supporting the corrugated roofs of the open pavilions are similarly encrusted with vertical rows of glass bottles.
*   **Mosaics and Art:** Intricate, colorful mosaics decorate the walls. Instead of traditional tile, these artworks—depicting religious imagery such as a seated Buddha alongside an elephant and a monkey—are crafted entirely from thousands of recycled metallic bottle caps pressed into cement.

#### The Environment and Layout
*   **The Water Basin:** A large, static, rectangular basin of green-tinted water surrounds the base of the main temple, isolating it from the main courtyard and creating a cool, reflective boundary.
*   **Courtyards and Corridors:** Wide, grey concrete pathways and parking areas connect the various structures. Stationary vehicles (pickup trucks) and temporary blue-and-yellow tent structures are visible in the surrounding lots.
*   **Vegetation:** The complex is heavily interspersed with and surrounded by mature, leafy tropical trees and tall deciduous species, providing a dense, natural green canopy that contrasts with the glass-and-concrete architecture.

#### Human and Biological Presence
*   **The Monks:** The site is maintained and inhabited by resident Buddhist monks, identifiable by their traditional bright saffron/orange robes, representing a calm, focused biological presence amidst the recycled architecture.
*   **Visitors:** As an approved eco-friendly sightseeing destination, the site accommodates tourists, supported by infrastructure like the bottle-lined public restrooms.
