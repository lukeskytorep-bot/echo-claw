# Target 0045

**Target ID:** 812S LK55  
**Target coordinates:** 07°57′25″N 80°45′35″E (Sigiriya, Matale District, Sri Lanka)  
**Date of the target:** 5th Century CE (King Kashyapa's reign, 477–495 CE) / Present-day archaeological site  

**Target:** The ancient rock fortress, palace ruins, and landscaped gardens of Sigiriya (Lion Rock).

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of an ancient, monumental urban planning and fortress complex built around a massive geological monolith. The site is characterized by the stark contrast between a towering, sheer-sided granite outcrop and the highly ordered, symmetrical, and geometrically planned water gardens laid out at its base, all surrounded by dense tropical forest.

---

### Part 1: The Geological Monolith and Upper Citadel

*   **The Rock Formation:** The central feature of the environment is a colossal, hardened granite monolith (an inselberg formed from a volcanic magma plug) that rises abruptly approximately 180 meters (590 feet) above the surrounding flat plains.
*   **The Summit:** The top of the rock is entirely flat and heavily modified by human engineering. It contains the exposed brick foundations and terraced ruins of an ancient upper palace complex, including large geometric water cisterns cut directly into the stone.
*   **The Drop:** The sides of the rock are sheer, near-vertical cliffs, heavily weathered and streaked with dark and light mineral deposits.

### Part 2: The Mid-Level Terrace and Rock Face

*   **The Lion Gate:** Located on a small plateau roughly halfway up the northern side of the rock is a monumental entrance. The gateway is flanked by two massive, sculpted stone lion's paws with prominent claws, between which a steep staircase ascends toward the summit.
*   **The Mirror Wall:** Along the western face of the rock is a pedestrian pathway bordered by a brick masonry wall. This wall is covered in a highly polished, yellowish-white plaster, bearing centuries of ancient, etched graffiti and poetry.
*   **The Frescoes:** Sheltered in a rock overhang above the Mirror Wall are highly detailed, colorful painted frescoes. The paintings depict heavily adorned female figures (apsaras or celestial nymphs) wearing elaborate jewelry and holding lotus flowers.

### Part 3: The Landscaped Gardens and Infrastructure

*   **The Water Gardens:** Stretching out from the western base of the rock is a vast, symmetrical landscaped park. It features a central linear pathway that bisects a series of perfectly rectangular, brick-lined pools, deep cisterns, and serpentine streams connected by an underground hydraulic conduit system.
*   **The Boulder and Terraced Gardens:** Transitioning from the flat water gardens to the base of the rock are asymmetrical, winding pathways navigating through massive natural boulders, leading up to concentric, stepped brick terraces built into the hillside.
*   **The Environment:** The highly manicured, geometric ruins of the city and gardens are completely encircled by a dense, lush, green canopy of tropical forest.
