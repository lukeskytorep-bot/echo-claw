# Target 0044

**Target ID:** 881C MX55  
**Date of the target:** April 2000  

**Target:** The Cave of Crystals (Crystal Cave of Giants) located within the Naica Mine, Chihuahua, Mexico.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of an extreme subterranean geological environment in the Naica Mine of Chihuahua, Mexico. The scene captures a massive underground void filled with some of the largest natural crystals ever discovered on Earth, accessed via a deep industrial mining operation.

---

### Part 1: The Geological Formations (The Crystals)

*   The central focus is a dense, chaotic accumulation of colossal selenite (gypsum) crystals. 
*   These translucent, white structures take the shape of massive, faceted pillars and sharp, sword-like beams protruding from all angles of the cave walls and floor. 
*   The largest crystals reach up to 50 feet in length and 4 feet in diameter, dwarfing human explorers. 
*   They are formed by mineral-rich hydrothermal fluids and grew extremely slowly, taking up to a million years to form.

### Part 2: The Subterranean Environment

*   The cavern is located approximately 1,000 feet below the surface, encased within the limestone host rock of an active lead, zinc, and silver mine. 
*   The cavern walls consist of rough, reddish-brown bedrock. 
*   The environment is characterized by extreme, life-threatening heat generated by a magma chamber located two to three miles beneath the mountain.

### Part 3: Human Exploration and Scale

*   Human presence in the cave is strictly limited by the "blast furnace" temperatures and high humidity, which can quickly cause loss of consciousness. 
*   Explorers must wear specialized gear, including helmets, headlamps, rubber boots, and gloves. 
*   In visual data, human figures wearing protective clothing carefully balance and climb across the massive, crisscrossing crystal beams, providing a stark sense of the cavern's immense scale.
