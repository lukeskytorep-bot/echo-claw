# Target: Traditional Production of Ojiya Hemp Fabrics (Ojiya Chijimi)

The presented materials illustrate a unique, multi-step process of manually producing exclusive hemp fabrics in Ojiya City (Niigata Prefecture, Japan). This craft, dating back to the 17th century, is inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list and serves as a prime example of the collaboration between human labor and natural elements.

## Fabric Production Stages:

* **Yarn Preparation (Manual Tying):** The process begins with extracting fibers from harvested hemp plants. Since individual fibers are short, craftsmen precisely and painstakingly tie them together by hand to create long threads ready to be wound onto spindles.
* **Weaving:** The resulting yarn is sent to traditional, manually operated wooden looms, where it is carefully woven into raw material.
* **Design and Dyeing:** The fabric undergoes dyeing or hand-painting to achieve desired, often highly intricate patterns.
* **Kneading and Softening (Water Processing):** To remove the natural coarseness of hemp, the material is subjected to intensive physical treatment. Craftsmen knead the fabric with their feet in tubs of water (resembling traditional grape treading) and then repeatedly soak, wring, and work it by hand in hot water, which gives it softness and a unique texture.
* **Snow Bleaching (Yuki-zarashi):** This is the most visually striking stage of the process, taking place in mid-winter. Long rolls of material are spread directly onto snow-covered fields. The action of the sun and melting snow naturally brightens and sets the colors, while specific temperature conditions give the fabric its final, slightly wrinkled texture.

## Finished Product and Significance:

* **Final Product (High Fashion):** The prepared material is used to sew luxury summer kimonos, cushion covers, and bedding.
* **Cultural and Functional Importance:** Although the production process occurs in harsh winter conditions, the finished fabric is famous for its extreme lightness, breathability, and cooling properties—ideal for hot Japanese summers. Kimonos made from Ojiya hemp are highly prized, often exceeding the value and prestige of those made from pure silk.
