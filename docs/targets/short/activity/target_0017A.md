# Target: Cluster Balloon Lawn Chair Flight (Bend, Oregon to Cambridge, Idaho (USA))

The presented target depicts the successful cluster balloon flight undertaken by Kent Couch on July 5, 2008. Inspired by childhood dreams and following two prior partially successful attempts, Couch utilized a lawn chair attached to a large array of giant helium-filled weather balloons to fly from his gas station in Bend, Oregon, across the desert border to a safe landing in Cambridge, Idaho.

## Aircraft Construction and Infrastructure:

The amateur aircraft, dubbed the "Kent Flying Machine," relies entirely on fundamental buoyancy principles rather than traditional mechanical propulsion.
* **The Flight Rig:** The core of the aircraft is a standard green folding lawn chair mounted onto a custom-built frame constructed from lightweight PVC piping. Attached to the structure are small American flags and technical instruments used by the pilot.
* **The Balloon Cluster:** A dense, massive cloud of approximately 150 giant, multi-colored weather balloons (predominantly red, blue, yellow, and white) provides the necessary lift to overcome gravity. 
* **The Ballast System:** To control vertical ascent and descent, large plastic cylinders (labeled "UNICEL") are strapped underneath the PVC frame. These containers carry a liquid ballast (specifically cherry-flavored Kool-Aid), which can be progressively siphoned or drained to lighten the rig and cause it to rise. Conversely, the pilot carries a handheld BB gun to carefully shoot out individual balloons when he needs to descend or land.

## Launch Site and Flight Environment:

* **The Launch Base:** The initial staging and assembly take place at dawn on a flat, asphalt lot or parking area adjacent to local commercial buildings (including a structure with a prominent "JOE'S" sign). 
* **Flight Atmosphere:** Once airborne, the cluster balloon rig drifts into a wide, open sky characterized by sweeping cumulus cloud formations. The flight path depends completely on ambient wind currents for directional navigation, taking approximately nine hours to cover a distance of about 235 miles at varying low altitudes.

## Human Activity and Logistics:

* **Ground Support:** The preparation phase involves a large team of ground volunteers, many dressed in matching bright green shirts, working to fill the array of giant balloons with helium. 
* **The Launch (Push-off):** Because of the weighted balance of the liquid ballast, the ground crew manually lifts and pushes the rig up into the air during liftoff. This ensures the pilot safely clears nearby utility power lines before the balloons gain autonomous altitude.
* **Spectators and Arrival:** The launch draws a significant crowd of onlookers and media personnel capturing the departure with professional video cameras. The flight concludes with a safe pasture touchdown in the small farming community of Cambridge, Idaho, drawing out nearly half the local town to greet the pilot.
