### 1. Detailed Description (Historical & Physical Facts)
The target captures the precise moment a massive cargo vessel collided with a critical infrastructure bridge in Baltimore, Maryland. The Francis Scott Key Bridge was a large, continuous through-truss bridge made of heavy steel, spanning the lower Patapsco River and outer Baltimore Harbor.

At 1:28 AM, the MV Dali, a 300-meter-long (984 ft) container ship fully loaded with thousands of heavy shipping containers, suffered a total loss of power. Drifting off course at a speed of approximately 8 knots (15 km/h), the massive bow of the ship slammed directly into one of the bridge's main reinforced concrete support columns. 

The kinetic impact instantly destroyed the pier. Within seconds, the continuous steel truss structure lost its support and suffered a catastrophic, progressive collapse. Thousands of tons of steel framework, along with the concrete and asphalt roadway, buckled and plunged violently into the dark, freezing river below. A section of the heavy steel truss collapsed directly onto the bow of the container ship, pinning it in place. Several vehicles and a crew of construction workers who were repairing potholes on the bridge deck were thrown into the water amidst the falling debris, resulting in six fatalities.

There was no precipitation, but the absolute darkness and the thermal contrast between the massive metal structures and the cold water defined the physical space.
