**Description:**
The target is an indoor historical political event marking a symbolic moment in the fall of communism. It features Polish "Solidarity" leader Lech Wałęsa delivering a landmark address to the joint chambers of the United States Congress on November 15, 1989. ### Detailed Scene Breakdown

#### The Speaker (Lech Wałęsa)
*   Wałęsa is positioned at the main wooden podium at the center of the chamber.
*   He is wearing a formal dark suit, a dark tie, and a light shirt, with his distinctive mustache clearly visible.
*   A large, rectangular "Solidarność" (Solidarity) logo pin is prominently attached to his left lapel.
*   He reads intently from prepared notes resting on the lectern, speaking into a thick black microphone stand.

#### The Speech and Message
*   The address famously began with the English words drawn directly from the US Constitution: "We, the People" ("My, naród").
*   Wałęsa stated he came from a Polish land that was "tormented but unbroken," a declaration that prompted a massive standing ovation from the assembly.
*   The core intent of his speech was an urgent appeal for the United States to provide economic support for the emerging democratic transformations in Poland and Central-Eastern Europe.
*   His presence held immense symbolic weight, as he was only the third foreigner in history to address the joint Congress without holding the official title of head of state or government.

#### The Chamber and Atmosphere
*   The architecture of the room consists of a large, semi-circular amphitheater-style chamber featuring tiered, curved wooden desks.
*   The floor is covered with a highly patterned blue and red carpet.
*   The chamber is filled to absolute capacity with attendees, including politicians and high-ranking military officials in formal suits and uniforms. 
*   The atmosphere is energetic and highly supportive, with attendees standing, smiling, and clapping enthusiastically as Wałęsa arrives at the rostrum.
