# Target: Bingham Canyon Open-Pit Mine

The presented materials document the Bingham Canyon Mine (also known as the Kennecott Copper Mine) – located in the Oquirrh Mountains of Utah, the world’s deepest open-pit copper mine. This operation has been active since 1906, and due to its immense size and characteristics, it exceeds the standard framework of landscape perception.

## Structure and Target Environment:

* **Target Center:** This is a unique environment where the center is defined by the site of the greatest change over time rather than the largest mass itself. It is a giant, man-made hollow in the land (over 1.2 km deep and 4 km wide) that has drastically transformed the original geological formation. The mountain itself—typically a steep and dominant landform—has been systematically deconstructed and inverted into the ground.
* **Roads and Spatial Layout:** Stepped terraces carved into the rock create an extensive communication network. The roads here are massive, horizontal strips of matter firmly embedded in the ground, spiraling down to the very bottom of the excavation. Due to the scale of the space, this infrastructure requires street lighting all the way to the floor of the pit.

## Movement and Matter:

* **Artificial Objects (Machinery):** The giant haul trucks (such as the CAT models) and loaders operating in the mine are distinctly artificial, hard objects constructed of steel. Their work and the extraction processes generate a steady, industrial rhythm. From the perspective of the observation platform at the edge of the crater, these massive machines appear tiny, resembling toy trucks in a sandbox.
* **Dynamics on Routes:** The movement of vehicles traveling along the cascading descents appears as a sequence of movements occurring one after another along the lines designated on the massive roads.
* **Presence of People:** Human presence can be observed in designated visitor zones (the visitor center, viewing platforms, and the exhibit featuring a giant tire). In this highly industrial and hard environment, people stand out distinctly against the cold, massive background of the mine.

## Observational Context:

The entire area is a space of continuous, intentional land transformation. Recording all visual anomalies is crucial, as they are an integral part of this man-made target. These include the geometric repetition of hundreds of carved terraces and clusters of unnaturally colored process water at the bottom of the excavation.
