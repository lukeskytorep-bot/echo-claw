*   **Target:** The Hindenburg airship disaster (LZ 129).

---

### 1. Detailed Description (Historical & Physical Facts)
The target captures the precise moment of the destruction of the German passenger airship Hindenburg (LZ 129) during its docking attempt at the Naval Air Station Lakehurst in New Jersey, USA.

The airship was a colossal structure, measuring 245 meters (804 feet) in length and about 41 meters (135 feet) in diameter. Its rigid framework was constructed of a duraluminium alloy, covered by a cotton fabric skin treated with iron oxide and cellulose acetate butyrate dope. The interior housed 16 massive gas cells filled with highly flammable hydrogen gas, totaling over 200,000 cubic meters. A control car and passenger quarters—complete with cabins, a dining room, a lounge, and promenade windows—were located within the lower portion of the hull.

At 7:25 PM, shortly after dropping its mooring lines in a light rain, a fire broke out near the stern (tail section) of the airship. The hydrogen ignited instantly. Within 32 to 37 seconds, the entire vessel was consumed by flames and crashed to the ground. Out of the 97 people on board (36 passengers and 61 crew members), 35 perished, alongside one ground crew worker on the airfield floor. People scrambled to save themselves by leaping from the windows of the passenger deck from heights of several stories while the burning framework buckled around them and molten metal rained down. On the ground, absolute chaos erupted as dozens of ground crew members, reporters, and onlookers fled in all directions to escape the falling, blazing mass.

---

### 2. Environment & Weather
The event occurred in the late afternoon under overcast skies. A thunderstorm had recently passed over the area, leaving the air highly humid with a light drizzle and variable winds. The surrounding area of Naval Air Station Lakehurst was a vast, flat, sandy, and grassy airfield dominated by a massive steel mooring mast.

---

### 3. Spatial Layout & Map Outline
*   **The Airfield Base:** A broad, flat, and dry open field. There are no hills or bodies of water in the immediate vicinity of the crash site.
*   **Fixed Structures:** A high steel mooring mast stands prominently on the field. Massive airship hangars are located further in the distance.
*   **Crash Dynamics:** The airship was roughly 60 meters (200 feet) in the air when the fire erupted at the stern. Because the rear gas cells failed first, the tail of the airship struck the ground heavily while the bow angled steeplo into the air. Flames erupted forward through the hull like a chimney before the entire structure flattened and collapsed into a smoking heap of twisted metal on the ground.
