# Target: Honey Harvesting and Extraction Process

The presented target is a multi-step agricultural and production activity focused on harvesting honey. The process involves interacting with live organisms in their natural habitat, followed by the mechanical extraction of the gathered material indoors. 

## Detailed Mechanics and Activity Flow:

**Phase 1: Outdoor Extraction**
1. **Accessing the Hive:** The activity begins outdoors in a grassy environment. A person wearing protective beekeeping gear, including thick gloves, opens a wooden beehive box.
2. **Frame Removal:** Grasping the top bar with both hands, the person carefully pulls a rectangular wooden frame vertically out of the hive. The frame is densely covered with a hexagonal wax honeycomb structure and a heavily active swarm of live bees. 
3. **Examination:** The person holds the frame up to examine the honeycomb. They are checking to see if the hexagonal cells are fully loaded with honey and capped over with a protective layer of beeswax, which indicates the honey is ready for harvest.

**Phase 2: Indoor Uncapping**
4. **Transition Indoors:** The honey-filled frames (now cleared of bees) are moved to an indoor, structured environment with tiled floors, indicating a dedicated processing area.
5. **Uncapping the Cells:** The person holds the wooden frame at an angle over a square metal catch-tray. Using a specialized hand tool (an uncapping fork or scraper), they systematically scrape and peel away the thin top layer of wax caps sealing the honey inside the cells. The sticky, discarded wax clumps fall directly into the metal tray below, exposing the liquid honey inside the comb.

**Phase 3: Mechanical Extraction**
6. **Loading the Centrifuge:** The uncapped frames are then placed inside a honey extractor—a large, cylindrical metal machine built specifically for this purpose. The frames are slotted vertically into a rotating cage or basket mechanism positioned around a central metal axis.
7. **Spinning the Frames:** The internal cage is set into a rapid spinning motion. As the frames rotate, centrifugal force pulls the thick, viscous honey out of the open hexagonal cells and flings it against the inner stainless-steel walls of the cylindrical drum.
8. **Draining the Honey:** The extracted honey slowly drips down the inner walls and pools at the bottom of the extractor. A mechanical gate valve located at the base of the machine is opened, allowing the heavy, golden liquid honey to pour out in a thick, continuous, smooth stream into a separate container for collection.
