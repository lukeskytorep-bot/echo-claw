# Target: Low Earth Orbiting International Space Station (Early Assembly Phase)

The presented target is the International Space Station (ISS) captured in its early structural assembly phase during a fly-around maneuver by the Space Shuttle Atlantis during the STS-106 mission. The complex is drifting in low Earth orbit, serving as a permanent microgravity research laboratory.

## Object Structure and Architecture:

* **Modular Configuration:** The target shows the early configuration of the station, primarily consisting of connected cylindrical pressurized modules arranged in a linear fashion. This early structure prominently features the Russian Zvezda Service Module, the Zarya Functional Cargo Block (FGB), and the American Unity Node.
* **Solar Arrays:** Extending from the central modules are two pairs of dark, rectangular solar array panels deployed symmetrically. These solar arrays are designed to capture solar energy to power the early on-board systems and life-support mechanisms of the station.
* **Technical Details:** The exterior is covered in a mix of white thermal blankets and metallic shielding. Multiple docking ports, communication antennas, and structural trusses are visible along the spine of the connected modules.

## Environment and Background:

* **Earth Backdrop:** The space station is positioned against a striking backdrop of planet Earth. The planet's surface is heavily covered with complex, sweeping formations of white clouds, contrasting sharply with the deep blue color of the oceanic or land masses beneath.
* **Atmosphere:** The image captures the high-altitude perspective of low Earth orbit, emphasizing the vast, open vacuum of space directly adjacent to the thin, blue atmospheric layer of the planet.
