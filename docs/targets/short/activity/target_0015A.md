# Target: 1999 Bourbonnais Train Crash (Illinois, USA)

The presented target is the scene of a major transportation accident involving Amtrak's southbound "City of New Orleans" passenger train. The disaster occurred at a grade crossing in the village of Bourbonnais, Illinois, resulting in a massive derailment and significant loss of life.

## Incident Scale and Infrastructure:

* **Derailment Structure:** The aerial view reveals a severe, jagged pile-up of passenger cars. The force of the impact caused the train to buckle violently, leaving silver passenger cars strewn across the tracks in a sharp "Z" or zig-zag pattern. 
* **Wreckage Layout:** Eleven of the train's fourteen cars, along with the two leading locomotives, left the tracks. Several cars are seen tilted precariously, while others collided directly with stationary freight cars located on an adjacent siding track. 
* **Surrounding Terrain:** The crash site is situated in an open, flat landscape bordered by sparse trees and dry vegetation. In the lower-left corner, a public road crosses the railroad tracks, marking the grade crossing where the initial collision took place.

## Logistics and Responders:

* **Vehicles at the Scene:** Near the crossing at the bottom of the frame, a large white semi-trailer truck is visible on the road running parallel to the tracks. 
* **Personnel:** Small figures of response personnel and investigators are scattered around the base of the derailed cars, particularly near the rear car closest to the grade crossing, assessing the structural damage and initiating recovery operations.

## Event Background:

* **Cause and Impact:** The crash occurred at approximately 9:45 p.m. local time when the passenger train, traveling at 79 mph (127 km/h), collided with a flatbed truck loaded with steel that was attempting to clear the tracks. 
* **Casualties:** The collision killed eleven people and injured another 121 passengers and crew members, leading to more than US$14 million in damages and resulting in revised National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) recommendations for grade crossing safety.
