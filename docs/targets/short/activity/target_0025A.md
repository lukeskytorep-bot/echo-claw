## TRAINING TARGET 025A

*   **TRN (Identifier):** `4418v xzt2`
*   **Target Date and Time:** October 9, 1963, 10:39 PM local time.
*   **Coordinates (GPS):** 46°16'02.0"N, 12°19'44.0"E
*   **Target:** The Vajont Dam disaster – a massive landslide and subsequent megatsunami striking the Longarone valley.

---

### 1. Detailed Description (Historical & Physical Facts)
The target captures the exact moment of a catastrophic hydrotechnical disaster in northern Italy. The Vajont Dam was one of the tallest dams in the world at the time—a double-curvature concrete arch dam standing 262 meters high, wedged into an extremely narrow, nearly vertical rocky gorge. A massive artificial reservoir lay directly behind it.

At 10:39 PM, a colossal landslide detached from the southern slope of Monte Toc, which towered directly over the lake. Approximately 260 million cubic meters of rock, earth, and forest collapsed into the reservoir in a single, solid block at speeds reaching 110 km/h.

The plunging mass of earth instantly displaced the water, creating a megatsunami approximately 250 meters high. This wall of water crashed into the opposite slope and then overtopped the crest of the concrete dam. The dam structure itself survived this immense impact completely intact. However, a wall of 50 million cubic meters of water rushed down the steep gorge with tremendous force. The roaring water was preceded by a massive shockwave of compressed air (hurricane-force winds) that struck the Piave River valley before the water even arrived. The town of Longarone and several surrounding villages at the base of the mountain were literally wiped off the map within minutes. Nearly 2,000 people lost their lives, and all structures were reduced to a flat expanse of mud and debris.

---

### 2. Environment & Weather
The event occurred in the middle of the night under complete darkness due to heavy cloud cover and the late hour. Continuous, intense rainfall had plagued the region for several days, soaking the ground and ultimately triggering the landslide. The surrounding terrain is a steep, alpine landscape (the Belluno Dolomites) characterized by sharp rocky peaks and dense vegetation.

---

### 3. Spatial Layout & Map Outline
*   **East / Background (Landslide & Reservoir):** A massive, towering mountain (Monte Toc) with an entire section of its slope collapsing. Below, a colossal mass of earth slams into the water, forcing it upward and outward, displacing the reservoir with mud and stone.
*   **Center (The Dam):** A very tall, remarkably thin (only 3.4 meters thick at the top and 27 meters at the base), smooth concrete wall. Despite millions of tons of water violently cascading over its crest, the concrete barrier remains completely stationary and anchored into the canyon walls.
*   **West / Bottom (Valley of Destruction):** Beyond the dam, the terrain drops steeply into a narrow 200-meter gorge, which opens up into the wide, flat river valley below. Originally filled with human infrastructure (stone buildings, streets, factories), this entire area is transformed in an instant into a flat wasteland of thick mud, shattered trees, boulders, and rushing water, completely stripped of all architecture.
